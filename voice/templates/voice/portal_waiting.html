One step left, and it’s with your phone company

Your receptionist is ready on {{ program.answering_number }}. It cannot answer anything until your business line forwards to it, and that switch lives at your carrier rather than with us.

Call your carrier and ask for this:

{% if program.answer_mode == 'ring_you_first' %}
“Please forward {{ program.business_phone|default:"my business line" }} to {{ program.answering_number }} when I don’t answer, after about {{ program.ring_seconds }} seconds.”

That is called conditional forwarding. Your phone rings first exactly as it does now, and only the calls you miss reach the receptionist.

On the timing: we ask for {{ program.ring_seconds }} seconds, but that timer belongs to your carrier. Plenty of them hold it fixed nearer 20 to 25 seconds and a few will not change it at all. Whatever they say is what you will get, and we will tell you which once it is set.

{% else %}
“Please forward {{ program.business_phone|default:"my business line" }} to {{ program.answering_number }} for all calls.”

That is called unconditional forwarding. Worth knowing before you do it: your own handset will stop ringing for business calls. That is the point — it is what lets the receptionist screen the robocalls before they ever reach you — and real customers still get put straight through to {{ program.transfer_number|default:"you" }}.

{% endif %}

Most carriers do this in a couple of minutes on the phone. If you would rather we walked you through it, or did it with you on a call, reply to any of our emails and we will book ten minutes.
