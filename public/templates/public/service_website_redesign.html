The Risk Nobody Mentions

Why Most Website Redesigns Lose Traffic

Four failure modes, all preventable, all common.

The URLs Changed And Nothing Redirected

Your old /services/roof-repair became /what-we-do. Every link pointing at the old address now hits a 404, and years of accumulated authority evaporates. This is the single biggest cause of post-redesign traffic loss.

Pages That Were Working Got Deleted

A blog post from 2021 quietly brought in a third of your organic traffic. Nobody checked before the rebuild cut it for looking dated. You can’t protect what you never measured.

The New Site Is Prettier And Slower

Big hero videos, five webfonts, a slider nobody uses. It looks better in a screenshot and loses visitors on mobile data. Speed is a ranking factor and a conversion factor.

Nobody Recorded A Baseline

Traffic drops after launch. Was it the redesign, seasonality, or an algorithm update? Without before-numbers, nobody can tell — so nobody can fix it.

{# ───────── Process ───────── #}
How We Do It

Mapping First. Rebuild Second.

The first week of a redesign shouldn’t involve any design.

1. Full URL Inventory

Every page currently on the site, from the sitemap, Search Console and a crawl. You cannot redirect what you haven’t found, and there are always more pages than anyone expects.

2. Redirect Map

Each old URL mapped to its closest equivalent — one permanent redirect, no chains, no blanket redirect to the homepage. Pages with no real equivalent get an honest 404 rather than a misleading hop.

3. Baseline Recorded

Traffic, rankings, indexed pages and load speed captured before anything changes, so the effect of the redesign is measurable instead of arguable.

4. Content Triage

Which pages earn traffic, which convert, which are dead weight. Winners carry across. The rest get merged or dropped deliberately, not by accident.

5. Rebuild

Hand-coded, fast, security-hardened, mobile-first — the same build standard as any custom web design project.

6. Re-measure At 30 And 90 Days

We check the redirects resolved, coverage held, and speed improved — then compare against the baseline. If something slipped, we find it while it’s still fixable.

This is the same process we ran on our own site — URL inventory, redirect map, baseline, rebuild, re-measure. Mobile load time went from 4.1 seconds to 1.5. We didn’t guess at that; we measured it before and after.

{# ───────── When to redesign ───────── #}
Is It Time?

When A Redesign Is Worth It — And When It Isn’t

Worth It When

  • The site is slow on mobile and you’re losing people before they read anything
  • You don’t own it, or can’t change it without paying the vendor
  • It doesn’t work properly on a phone
  • The structure can’t support the pages you need to rank
  • It looks like it was built a decade ago, because it was

Probably Not Yet When

  • You just don’t like the colours — that’s a restyle, not a rebuild
  • Traffic is fine and the site converts
  • The real problem is that nobody can find you — that’s SEO, not design
  • You rebuilt it last year and nothing has actually changed since

Common Questions

Website Redesign Questions

Will a redesign hurt my rankings?

It can — that’s the most common failure. It happens when URLs change without redirects, ranking content gets cut, or the new site is slower. All three are avoidable, and avoiding them is most of what we do first.

How much does a redesign cost?

Same as a new build: $2,500–$4,500. A redesign isn’t cheaper — migrating content and preserving search equity is real work, and skipping it is what causes the drop.

Can I keep my existing content?

Yes, and usually you should. Pages that already earn traffic are assets. We find which ones work and carry them across instead of rewriting for the sake of it.

Do I have to change domain or hosting?

No. Keeping your domain is strongly preferred — that’s where your search history lives. If your current platform holds the registration, sorting that out is step one.

How long does it take?

About six weeks, plus a week up front for the URL inventory and redirect map. Sites with hundreds of pages take longer, mostly in the mapping.

What if I’m locked into a contract?

Check the term, the notice period, and who holds the domain. We can build in parallel and cut over when you’re free. For law firms specifically, see for law firms.
