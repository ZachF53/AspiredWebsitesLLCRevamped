Switching

What Actually Happens When You Leave

The five questions every firm asks before making the move — answered before you have to ask them.

Am I locked into a contract?

Check the term and the notice period on your current agreement first — many are annual with 30 to 60 days notice. You don’t have to wait to start: we can build in parallel and cut over the day you’re free, so you lose nothing but the overlap.

Who owns my domain?

Check this today, before anything else. It’s where switches get stuck. If your vendor holds the registration, request a transfer early — it can take days. Your domain is where your entire search history lives; you should hold it in your own name, and with us you always do.

Can I take my content?

Content you wrote or paid to have written is normally yours — check your agreement. We migrate what you own and rewrite what we can’t take. Practice-area pages that already rank are assets, so we carry them across rather than starting from zero.

Will my site go down?

No. The new site is built and tested on a staging URL while your current one stays live. The switch is a DNS change at the end — minutes, not days, and scheduled when you choose.

Will I lose my Google rankings?

Not if the migration is done properly, and this is the part most rebuilds get wrong. We inventory every existing URL and map each one to its closest equivalent with a single permanent redirect, so links and rankings follow you. We also record your traffic and rankings before launch, so any change is measurable rather than arguable.

How long does the whole thing take?

About six weeks to build, plus a week up front for the URL mapping. Most of the calendar is content and review, not code — and you always know which stage you’re in.

Still weighing it up? Book a call and we’ll look at your current contract and domain setup with you — no obligation, and we’ll tell you if staying put is the better move.
